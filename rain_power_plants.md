# ☢️ What If: Nuclear Rain

### ❓ The Question

If we replaced all global energy production with nuclear power, how much "extra" rainfall would be generated by the steam billowing out of the cooling towers? Would we all need to carry umbrellas near power plants?

---

### 🧮 The Math

To get a handle on this, we first need to look at how much water a single nuclear plant "exhales."

* **The Unit Plant:** A standard 1,000 MW (1 GW) nuclear plant using wet cooling towers evaporates roughly **2.7 million gallons** of water per day (about 10,000 cubic meters).
* **Global Energy Needs:** Total human primary energy consumption is roughly **160,000 TWh** per year. If we assume a global nuclear fleet providing all that energy (with an average thermal efficiency of ~33%), we're looking at a staggering amount of waste heat.
* **The Total Steam:** Converting total global energy production to nuclear would result in roughly **334 cubic kilometers (km³)** of water evaporated annually.

---

### ⚖️ The Scale Comparison

To understand if 334 km³ is "a lot," we have to compare it to the "Natural Rainfall Machine" (the Earth):

| Feature | Volume |
| --- | --- |
| **Total Atmospheric Water** | 13,000 km³ |
| **Total Annual Global Precipitation** | 505,000 km³ |
| **Nuclear Steam Contribution** | **~0.06% of annual rainfall** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> In other words, if you took all the "nuclear rain" and spread it evenly across the planet, it would add about **0.6 millimeters** to the annual global rainfall. For context, a single "light drizzle" usually drops about 1 millimeter per hour.

---

### 🌬️ The "What If" Scenarios

#### 1. The Localized Monsoon 🌦️

While the global impact is negligible, the local impact is not. If you stand downwind of a massive cooling tower, you aren't just seeing steam; you're seeing a localized weather system.

* **Industrial Fog:** Cooling towers can increase local humidity enough to create "persistent ground fog."
* **Power Plant Snow:** In freezing temperatures, these plumes can actually trigger "lake-effect snow." This has been documented in places like Pennsylvania and France, where towns downwind of a plant get an extra inch of snow that their neighbors don't.

#### 2. The Great Humidity Bump 🌡️

Water vapor is a potent greenhouse gas. However, unlike $CO_2$, water vapor follows a "first-in, first-out" policy. If a nuclear plant emits a molecule of water, it usually falls as rain within **9 to 10 days**.

---

### 🏁 The Verdict

If we switched entirely to nuclear power:

* **Global level:** You wouldn't notice. The natural variation in rainfall from year to year is roughly 100 times larger than the total output of our hypothetical nuclear fleet.
* **Local level:** If you live within 5 miles of a cooling tower, you might want to invest in slightly better windshield wipers and prepare for the occasional **"nuclear snow day."**

**Conclusion:** We are currently being "out-rained" by the Sun's evaporation of the oceans by a factor of about **1,500 to 1**.
